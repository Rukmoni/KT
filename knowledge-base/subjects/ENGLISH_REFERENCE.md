### 🔴 FLAMINGO — PROSE

---

#### 1. THE LAST LESSON (Alphonse Daudet)
**Theme:** Patriotism, value of mother tongue, loss, identity
**Characters:** Franz (narrator, school student), M. Hamel (French teacher)
**Setting:** Alsace, France under Prussian/German occupation

**Key Points:**
- Franz fears scolding for missing French lesson, finds strange quiet in school
- M. Hamel wearing best clothes — bad omen
- Prussians have ordered only German to be taught in schools
- This is the LAST French lesson
- M. Hamel praises French language: "most beautiful, clearest, most logical"
- Franz realises he never truly valued French before — now it's being taken away
- Old villagers come to pay respects — they feel guilty for not learning more
- M. Hamel, overcome with emotion, writes "VIVE LA FRANCE!" on the board and dismisses class

**Important Themes:**
- Imperialism/colonialism destroys cultural identity
- We value things only when we lose them
- Language = identity of a people
- Importance of mother tongue

**PYQ Extract Pattern:**
> "That is what I thought when my friend Wachter called..." / "Then, from one thing to another..."
- Sub-questions on: character analysis, inference, vocabulary, author's purpose

**Short Answer Questions (likely):**
- Why did Franz fear going to school that morning? (He hadn't learned his lesson in French participles)
- What did M. Hamel mean by "always put off learning"?
- What feelings does Franz have about his French learning at the end?

#### 2. LOST SPRING (Anees Jung)
**Theme:** Poverty, child labour, exploitation, denial of childhood
**Two Stories:**
1. **Saheb-e-Alam** — ragpicker boy in Delhi (from Dhaka)
2. **Mukesh** — bangle maker in Firozabad

**Key Points — Saheb:**
- Name means "Lord of the Universe" — ironic
- Picks garbage (ragpicking) to survive
- Barefoot: "Looking for gold in the garbage"
- Spring = metaphor for childhood/hope
- Author offers school → Saheb happy → but nothing changes
- Saheb gets job at tea stall → loses freedom, is now a "servant"

**Key Points — Mukesh:**
- Family in Firozabad makes bangles (glass work) for generations
- Family SHOULD be trapped — but Mukesh WANTS to be a motor mechanic
- Tradition vs. aspiration — Mukesh dares to dream
- Saheb has no dream; Mukesh has ONE dream
- Vicious cycle: debt → no money for school → continued poverty → more debt

**PYQ Pattern:** Extract from either part. Comparison between Saheb and Mukesh very common in long answers.

#### 3. DEEP WATER (William Douglas)
**Theme:** Fear, overcoming fear, determination, willpower
**Autobiographical narrative — Douglas learns to swim**

**Key Incidents:**
1. YMCA pool — boy pushed him into deep end; nearly drowned
2. Yakima river — wave knocked him down at age 3–4; began fear of water
3. As adult hires instructor; learns swimming gradually over months
4. Tests himself: Lake Wentworth, then Warm Lake (Canada)
5. Finally conquers fear completely

**Key Quotes:**
- "I had previously experienced drowning..." (flashback to Yakima)
- "All my old terror returned"
- He felt fear completely leave: "I was able to add to life's experiences"
- Tillich quote: "The only way to overcome death is to face it"

**Important Themes:**
- Irrational fears can be conquered through determination
- Childhood trauma has lasting effects
- Water = symbol of fear AND life
- Methodical approach + will = victory

**Short Answer Pattern:**
- What lesson can Douglas's experience teach about overcoming fear?
  → That fears, however irrational, can be overcome through persistent effort and facing them head-on.

#### 4. THE RATTRAP (Selma Lagerlöf)
**Theme:** Human goodness, redemption, kindness changes people
**Characters:** Peddler (sells rattraps), Edla (ironmaster's daughter)

**Key Points:**
- Peddler's philosophy: "The world is a rattrap"
- He steals from the old crofter who showed him kindness
- Takes shelter at ironmaster's forge; mistaken for an old friend (Nils Olof)
- Edla invites him home despite knowing he's not Nils Olof
- Her genuine warmth and trust transforms him
- He leaves a package with: the stolen money, a rattrap, and a note
- Signs himself "Captain von Stahle" — has redeemed himself

**Key Quotes:**
- "She said this in such a friendly manner..." (SQP extract — appeared 3/6 times)
- The rattrap = symbol of temptations that trap people
- His gift = symbol of return to goodness

**PYQ Extract Questions:**
- Why does the peddler leave the gift? (Edla's kindness redeemed him)
- What does "rattrap" symbolise in the story?
- Character of Edla: compassionate, non-judgmental, sees good in others

#### 5. INDIGO (Louis Fischer, about Gandhi)
**Theme:** Non-violent resistance, justice, championing the poor
**Setting:** Champaran, Bihar — indigo farmers vs. British planters

**Key Points:**
- Champaran farmers forced to grow indigo on 15% of land; couldn't change
- When synthetic indigo appeared, planters wanted sharecroppers to pay to be released from the obligation
- Gandhi comes to Champaran despite opposition, lawyer, lieutenant pressure
- Refuses to leave until investigation done
- British forced to refund some money — Gandhi saw it as victory of justice
- Also set up schools and health centres — social reform alongside economic

**Important Theme for Long Answers:**
- "Courage and determination can force even the most powerful to yield"
- Comparison with Roadside Stand (both about exploitation of poor by rich)

#### 6. GOING PLACES (A.R. Barton)
**Theme:** Adolescent fantasy, hero worship, escapism, reality vs dreams
**Characters:** Sophie (narrator), Jansie, Danny, Geoff

**Key Points:**
- Sophie fantasises about meeting Irish football player Danny Casey
- Claims she actually met and talked to him — likely fantasy
- Jansie (realistic friend) is skeptical
- Sophie's family = working class; her dreams unrealistic for her circumstances
- Geoff = Sophie's brother; works as mechanic; Sophie envies his mysterious life
- The meeting with Danny Casey is ambiguous — likely imagined
- Theme: adolescent desire to escape limited circumstances through fantasy

**Common Exam Question:**
- Why does Jansie not share Sophie's dreams? (Jansie is practical, knows their class background)
- What does Sophie's fantasy reveal about her character? (escapism, hero worship, unrealistic expectations)

### 🔴 FLAMINGO — POETRY

---

#### 1. MY MOTHER AT SIXTY-SIX (Kamala Das)
**Theme:** Ageing, fear of separation, mother-daughter relationship, acceptance
- Poet's mother sleeping in car — looks pale like "late winter's moon"
- Fear of losing mother — compared to children's game-like quality of dealing with it
- At airport: says goodbye, smiling but holding back fear
- Does NOT say "don't worry" explicitly — smile = mask for grief
- "Familiar ache" = recurring fear of losing her mother
- "Young trees" and "merry children" = contrast to aging/death

---

#### 2. AN ELEMENTARY SCHOOL CLASSROOM IN A SLUM (Stephen Spender)
**Theme:** Poverty, inequality, denial of opportunity to poor children
- Children in slum school: "gusty waves", "fog", pallid faces — dehumanised
- Their cramped lives vs maps of a better world on the walls
- Maps on wall = IRONY — they are prisoners in their poverty
- "Unless, governor, inspector, visitor" — poet appeals to authority figures
- "Let the thick cortex of the world / burst open on these children's eyes"
- Theme: Education should liberate, not showcase what they can never have

---

#### 3. KEEPING QUIET (Pablo Neruda)
**Theme:** Introspection, stillness, peace, environmental consciousness
- Count to 12, stop all activity, keep complete silence
- NOT a silence of death or inactivity — but of reflection
- Would stop wars, pollution, environmental damage
- Fishermen, salt-gatherers, road workers — all should pause
- "Now I'll count to twelve / and you keep quiet and I will go"
- Earth is alive — it appears dead in winter but has life within (hope)

---

#### 4. A THING OF BEAUTY (John Keats)
**Theme:** Beauty is permanent, uplifting, source of joy
- Beautiful things: sun, moon, trees, daffodils, clear streams
- Beauty removes suffering from our "dark spirits"
- "An endless fountain of immortal drink"
- "The grandeur of the dooms we have imagined for the mighty dead"
- Heroes of old = beautiful memory
- Concept: Beauty never fades — it stays in memory and spirit

---

#### 5. A ROADSIDE STAND (Robert Frost)
**Theme:** Poverty, exploitation, inequality, urban-rural divide
- Poor rural people set up roadside stand to sell produce
- City people pass without stopping — too busy, too indifferent
- The "sorrow" of waiting for customers
- "Polished traffic" that "surges" past = wealth and indifference
- Contrast: rural poverty vs urban wealth
- Government housing scheme: would "sleep the poor" away — doesn't help
- Theme: Charity without understanding is condescending

---

#### 6. AUNT JENNIFER'S TIGERS (Adrienne Rich)
**Theme:** Female oppression, artistic freedom, patriarchal society
- Aunt Jennifer embroiders tigers: bright, fearless, prancing, unafraid of men
- She herself is terrified, fingers flutter under "uncle's wedding band" — heavy ring = oppression
- "Massive weight of uncle's wedding band" — marriage as burden/trap
- Tigers = what Aunt Jennifer wishes she could be (free, fearless)
- After her death: "ringed with ordeals she was mastered by" — still under oppression
- But the tigers will CONTINUE to prance — her art outlives her, expresses her true self

**SQP MCQ Pattern:**
- "The tigers in the panel...will go on prancing" — what does poet imply?
  → That the tigers represent the free, fearless spirit Aunt Jennifer wished for, which outlasts her

### 🟡 VISTAS — SHORT STORIES

---

#### 1. THE THIRD LEVEL (Jack Finney)
**Theme:** Escapism, yearning for a simpler past, modern stress
- Charley finds a 3rd level at Grand Central Station (only 2 exist)
- Third level leads to 1894 (gaslit, slower, peaceful era)
- Psychiatrist friend calls it a "waking dream wish fulfillment"
- Charley wants to escape modern stress — through time
- Epilogue: friend SAM actually found the third level and is in 1894
- He mails a letter dated 1894 — suggests it might be real
- Theme: Past as escapism; modern anxiety drives people to fantasy

---

#### 2. THE ENEMY (Pearl S. Buck)
**Theme:** Duty vs. humanity, war, compassion, prejudice
- Dr. Sadao (Japanese) and wife Hana find a wounded American POW on beach
- He is the enemy — their country is at war with USA
- They face dilemma: hand him to military (death) or treat him (risk themselves)
- They treat him — humanity overrides nationalism
- General (Sadao's patient) promises to send assassins to kill the American, doesn't
- Sadao helps the POW escape by boat
- Theme: True humanity transcends war and politics

---

#### 3. SHOULD WIZARD HIT MOMMY? (John Updike)
**Theme:** Adult authority vs child's sense of justice, parental control, socialisation
- Father (Jack) tells daughter (Jo) a bedtime story about Roger Skunk
- Roger smells bad → Wizard gives him rose scent → friends like him
- Jo's expected ending: Roger's mother accepts the rose smell
- Jack's version: Mommy makes Wizard change smell back (to normal skunk smell)
- Jo protests: "Daddy, that's terrible!" — she sides with Roger
- Conflict: Jo's sense of fairness vs Jack's view that mothers know best
- Theme: Authority of parents in shaping children; societal conformity vs individuality

---

#### 4. ON THE FACE OF IT (Susan Hill) — DRAMA
**Characters:** Mr. Lamb (old man with tin leg, lives alone), Derry (14-year-old with acid-burned face)
**Theme:** Disability, loneliness, self-pity vs acceptance

**Key Points:**
- Derry enters Mr. Lamb's garden to be alone; Lamb catches him
- Derry has self-pity: "Nobody wants to see my face"
- Lamb: "Beauty is in the eye of the beholder"
- Lamb is crippled but lives fully: keeps garden, reads, welcomes all
- Lamb tells Derry about his positive outlook — people more afraid of him than he of them
- Derry goes home to tell mother he's going back — she discourages him
- When he returns: Lamb has fallen from ladder, is dead
- Derry weeps not for self but for Lamb — first real human connection

**Important Contrast:** Derry = self-pity, isolation; Lamb = acceptance, open to life
**Theme:** Disability is what you make of it; isolation is a choice; human connection heals

---

#### 5. EVANS TRIES AN O-LEVEL (Colin Dexter)
**Theme:** Wit, deception, cat-and-mouse, cleverness vs authority
- Evans (prisoner) requests to take German O-level exam
- Prison officials take elaborate precautions
- Evans appears to comply — but has secretly arranged escape
- Fake parson = Evans's friend
- Clues in question paper itself lead to his hideout (he had planted them)
- Governor tracks him down using clues in the paper
- Plot twist: Evans has ALREADY escaped again by the end
- Theme: Cleverness and planning can outsmart even strict security

### 🔴 WRITING SKILLS — EXACT FORMATS

#### NOTICE FORMAT:
```
SCHOOL NAME
NOTICE
Date: [DD Month YYYY]

HEADING IN CAPITAL LETTERS

Content in 2–3 sentences (what, when, where, who)

Name
Designation
```
**Rules:** Box format; official language; max 80 words; issued by authority

#### FORMAL LETTER FORMAT:
```
Sender's Address
Date

Receiver's Address/Designation

Subject: [Specific subject line]

Sir/Madam,

Opening paragraph: Purpose of letter
Body: Details (2 paragraphs for 6-mark question)
Closing: Request/expected action

Yours faithfully/sincerely,
[Signature]
Name
```
**Job Application must include:** skills, qualifications, experience, reason for applying
**Always include:** Subject line; appropriate salutation; formal tone

#### ARTICLE FORMAT:
```
HEADING (Bold/Underlined)
By [Name of Author]

Introductory paragraph (hook, introduce topic)
Body (2–3 paragraphs with examples/data/arguments)
Conclusion (solution/recommendation/reflection)
```
**Word limit:** 150–200 words for 6 marks

#### REPORT FORMAT:
```
REPORT ON [TOPIC]
[Event/Issue name]
Date: [Date of writing]
Reported by: [Name, Designation]

INTRODUCTION / BACKGROUND

FINDINGS / OBSERVATIONS

RECOMMENDATIONS

Signature
Name
```

## ⚡ READING SKILLS STRATEGIES

### For Passage 1 (12 marks — Factual/Discursive):
1. **Read questions FIRST** — then read passage with purpose
2. **Inference questions:** answers not directly stated — read between lines
3. **Vocabulary questions:** use context to guess meaning
4. **40-word answers:** write complete sentences; answer in EXACTLY the word limit

### For Passage 2 (10 marks — Data/Infographic):
1. Study graph/chart carefully — read all labels and legends
2. For "fill in the blank" with graph: cite specific data point
3. For "main purpose of survey" type: look for the research objective paragraph

### SQP Reading Q pattern:
- "Which textual evidence tells us..." → quote brief phrase
- "The writer emphasises X in all EXCEPT..." → EXCEPT format is common!
- "The main idea of the text is..." → MCQ format
- "Fill in the blank with suitable reason" → synthesis required

## 🎓 ANSWER WRITING TIPS FOR SAHANA

### For Extract-based Q9 (6 marks — 6 sub-questions):
1. Short precise answers — 1–2 sentences per sub-question
2. Vocabulary/reference questions: answer in FULL sentences
3. "What does this suggest about..." → character analysis
4. MCQs in extract: use process of elimination

### For Short Answer Q10 (2 marks each — 30–40 words):
1. One main point + one supporting detail
2. Begin with the answer, not with "The story says that..."
3. Stay within word limit — examiners do count

### For Long Answers Q12/Q13 (5 marks — 120–150 words):
1. **Structure:** Introduction (1 para) + Body/Evidence (2 paras) + Conclusion (1 para)
2. **Quotations:** Include 1–2 textual references
3. **Themes:** Always connect to main theme of the text
4. **Comparison questions:** Use a clear format — Text A says..., Text B says..., Similarity/Difference is...

### For Writing Skills:
1. **Format marks:** Always write correct format (heading, name, date, designation) — easy marks!
2. **Content marks:** Two paragraphs minimum for articles/letters
3. **Vocabulary marks:** Vary your words; avoid repetition
4. **Accuracy marks:** No grammatical errors; check spelling

### Keywords Examiners Look For:
- Literature: "symbolises", "implies", "conveys", "reflects", "contrasts with"
- Writing: "Furthermore", "Moreover", "However", "In conclusion", "It is imperative"
- Reading: "According to the passage", "The author suggests", "This is evident when"
